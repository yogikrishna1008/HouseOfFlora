# House of Flora — Photography Requirements

**Send this to whoever is shooting.** It covers everything the website needs, how each image gets used, and the technical specs.

---

## Summary

| Group | Images needed |
|---|---|
| Product shots | **3 per garment, minimum** (4–5 preferred) |
| Category images | 1 per category (can be reused from product shots) |
| Site / editorial images | 4 |
| Sharing preview image | 1 |
| Logo files | 3 formats |

**For a 12-piece launch that is roughly 45 images.** For a 20-piece launch, roughly 70.

---

## 1. Product Shots — 3 Per Garment Minimum

These are the ones that sell the clothes. Every garment needs all three.

### Shot A — Full front, on model *(required)*
The main catalogue image. Whole garment visible head to hem, model facing front, arms relaxed at the sides so the silhouette reads clearly.

> **This is the image customers judge the piece on.** If only one shot is perfect, make it this one.

### Shot B — Detail *(required)*
Close on whatever makes the piece worth its price — the print repeat, embroidery, the weave, a neckline, a button placket, the border. Shot close enough that the texture of the fabric is visible.

> **This is what the site shows when someone hovers over the product.** It should look clearly different from Shot A, not just a slight crop of it.

### Shot C — Movement or three-quarter *(required)*
Model turning, walking, or at a three-quarter angle. Shows how the garment falls and moves. Important for anything with drape — sarees, lehengas, anything gathered.

### Shot D — Back view *(strongly recommended)*
Especially for anything with a back detail, a tie, a zip, or a dupatta drape.

### Shot E — Flat lay *(optional)*
The garment laid flat on a plain surface. Useful for co-ords and sets where customers want to see both pieces separately.

---

## 2. Consistency Rules — The Important Part

Individual photo quality matters less than all of them matching. A catalogue of inconsistent images looks amateur even when each photo is good on its own.

- **Same background throughout.** One plain wall or seamless backdrop. Off-white, pale grey, or a soft neutral. Not pure white, not patterned, not a location.
- **Same lighting throughout.** Ideally soft daylight or one consistent studio setup. Shoot everything in one session if possible — light changes through the day.
- **Same model, or same model per category.** Switching models mid-catalogue makes the sizing look inconsistent.
- **Same camera distance and framing.** The garment should occupy roughly the same proportion of the frame in every Shot A.
- **Minimal styling.** No competing jewellery, bags or props. The garment is the subject.
- **Shoot vertical (portrait).** The whole site is built around tall images.

---

## 3. Technical Specs

| Setting | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Orientation | Vertical / portrait |
| Product shot ratio | **3:4** (e.g. 1200 × 1600px) |
| Minimum resolution | 2000px on the long edge |
| Format to deliver | JPG or PNG, highest quality, unedited or lightly edited |
| Colour | Accurate to the real garment — this matters more than making it look pretty |
| Focus | Sharp on the fabric. Soft-focus product shots are unusable |

**Do not** deliver images already compressed for social media or sent over WhatsApp. WhatsApp destroys image quality. Use Google Drive, Dropbox, or WeTransfer.

**Colour accuracy note:** if a garment is naturally dyed or hand printed, photograph it in neutral light and do not colour-grade it warmer or cooler. Customers return clothes that arrive a different shade than the photo, and that return costs more than the shoot did.

---

## 4. Category Images — 1 Each

One image representing each group. These can be pulled from the product shots.

- Kurta Sets
- Sarees
- Co-ords
- Dresses
- Lehengas

**Ratio: 3:4 vertical.** Pick the most visually striking piece in each group.

---

## 5. Site Images — 4 Total

| # | Where it appears | Ratio | What it should show |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Homepage hero** — the first thing anyone sees | **4:5** vertical | The single best image from the whole collection. Full garment, strong composition, room at the edges |
| 2 | **Story section** — mid-page | **5:4** horizontal | The making: cloth being printed, cut, or finished. Hands at work |
| 3 | **Workroom section** | **5:4** horizontal | The workspace, or a close detail of hand-finishing |
| 4 | **Sharing preview** — shown when the link is sent on WhatsApp or Instagram | **1200 × 630px** horizontal | Brand image with space for the logo. This is the first impression on every shared link |

> Image 4 matters more than it sounds. Most of your traffic will arrive through a WhatsApp forward or an Instagram link, and this is the thumbnail people see before deciding whether to tap.

---

## 6. Logo Files

- **SVG** — vector, for the site header *(most important)*
- **PNG with transparent background** — 1000px wide
- **Square version** — 512 × 512px, for the browser tab icon and social profiles

If the logo has a horizontal lockup *and* a stacked or icon-only version, send all of them.

---

## 7. Naming Convention

Name files so they can be matched to garments without guessing:

```
HOF-001-oleander-kurta-set-a-front.jpg
HOF-001-oleander-kurta-set-b-detail.jpg
HOF-001-oleander-kurta-set-c-movement.jpg
HOF-002-amaltas-lehenga-a-front.jpg
```

Unnamed files like `IMG_4471.jpg` mean someone has to open all 45 and work out which garment is which.

---

## 8. Deliver Alongside The Photos

Photography alone is not enough to build the product pages. For **each garment**, also send:

- [ ] Garment name
- [ ] Exact fabric composition — e.g. "100% cotton mul", "silk-cotton chanderi"
- [ ] Available colours, with the colour name you want shown on the site
- [ ] Available sizes
- [ ] **Actual stock count per size and colour**
- [ ] Selling price, and MRP if it is being shown as discounted
- [ ] Care instructions
- [ ] A one-line description and a longer paragraph, or notes we can write from
- [ ] Measurements per size — bust, waist, hip, length in inches

> **The measurements are not optional.** Fashion returns are driven almost entirely by fit. A correct size chart is the cheapest thing you can do to reduce them.

---

## 9. Priority If The Shoot Is Short On Time

If everything cannot be shot in one session, cut in this order:

1. **Shot A for every garment** — nothing can go live without this
2. **Shot B for every garment** — the hover image
3. **Homepage hero (image 1)**
4. Shot C for every garment
5. Category images — or reuse Shot A
6. Story and workroom images — or launch without them
7. Shot D and E

**Six garments shot properly will look better than twenty shot badly.** If the choice is between a thin catalogue and a rushed one, take the thin catalogue and add pieces weekly.
